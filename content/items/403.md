141, Orrong Road,\
Elsternwick S4\
Vic.\
Tuesday: 21st September, 1954.

Dearest Family,

Thank you so much,
Marg, for your so good letter, received
last evening. The big moment of
the day is looking in the letter box
when we get home, and I certainly
can't complain — I have a very fine
average. To-night, just to even
things up, we found two bills — gas
and E.L. Gas is much better —
£2.16.- for the month, & light 11/4d.
We have been more sparing with the
gas fire, which is the villain of the
piece.

Before I go any further, I
must thank Mother for the cigarettes
and the Edinburth Rock. There were
so many things that I left them

141, Orrong Road,\
Elsternwick S4\
Vic.\

Thursday: 28th October, 1954.

Dearling Dad,

A brief note whilst
forwarding a letter from Mother,
which Marg sent on and I
recieved this evening.

You should be packing
up by now, but no mention in
home letters of the date. I shall
be glad when you do — it is
too far away and time you
had some leisure.

You will be glad to
know that I still get up betimes
in the the morning and am at
work in good time — usually the
first in our section & {u|sometimes}
even the first in the building!
You can't believe it is true,

{{{page-break}}}

II

I expect.

Am going to Point Cook
for the week-end — the first time
since being in the flat. Had a
note from Brenda B last night
in which she says John has just
been promoted Squadron Leader.
A good cause of celebration.

Wish you could have had
some of the rain that fell here
this week. It was badly needed,
of course, but I personally could
have spared some. It just
fell gently but solidly for
two days & nights, with darly
a let-up. We had as much
in that time as generally falls
during the whole month.

Do hope you are not wearing
yourself out, darling, with
the wheat-weighing still to come.

I love you,

Joanee.

{{{page-break}}}

IX

scrub.

By the way, in case you care
my telephone number is — MXY130
(Albert Park Barracks), Extension 6563.
May be handy to have
until such time as we get the
'phone here.

I do wish you could see our
dear little flat. It really is home
now & I have unpacked everything
except one small case of summer
things & evening shoes, etc., which
I have no prospect of wearing.

We have heard of a shorthand
coach, & keep insisting that we
will ring up, but so far no action.
It is too nice at home. We
make roast dinner on Sunday
& now have a kitchen garden —
parsley, mint & chives, in three
pots on the back landing. By courtesy
of Viginia's middle son.

Miss you & love you all very much

Joanee.

{{{page-break}}}

II

a shade weary.

Sales on at present. We have again bought
very little, but there is still a lot of work to be done.

Mother came down to-day & mended
a hole in the pocket of your pants. Will fix the
place where the flannel is wearing on the pocket
next time.

Went in to see Cousin Ernest oh Saturday
afternoon. He averred he was "never better" & said
he had no pain at all. I believe Dr Minchin
took a piece of skin from his leg to graft in the
ear. I don't know what it was all about, but
apparently he had to remove quite a piece from
inside his ear. He said then he hoped to be
coming out about to-day, but I don't know
if he did. He said they treated him very well
& he had "too much attention" & plenty to eat.
Dear old chap.

Cousin Eva now says she won't come
to Perth until she has to — meaning, I suppose,
when she feels she can't look after herself any
more. It is bitter luck that one so independent
should lose her sight.
