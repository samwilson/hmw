## Transcription

"Woopenatty"\
{u|ARRINO.}\
11th October 194

The General Secretary


Dear Sir,

Herewith a mite towards the fight for freedom, accompanied
by a few constructive suggestions from an aged stockman who has
never been a member of a union, recognises the necessity for their
existence, yet deplores the ignorant, selfish, unpatriotic way in
which Unionism wields the power it has wrested from Capitalism,

In spite of Unionism's strength, the Moderates ean still
swing any Federal or State election, but they need to be convinced
of sincerity and honesty of purpose and here are some of their
lines of thought.

Menzies a brilliant orator with a rapier thrust in repartee,
yet nought but a hired professional talker who cannot lead us out
of the wilderness. They remember that he tried to engineer a Motor
Monopoly, bludgeon through the Dried Fruit Case thet was obviously
contrary to the Constitution and when in power never made a single
gesture towards State rights.

I know men who have been all their working lives financial
unionists, yet have most cynical disillusioned views of their
party and leaders, and under the lap would, although he is unproved,
support a Casey led Party on the principle of"try anything once".

A sense of frustration is abroad, end our side is partly to
blame.

For instance, the Bulletin boasts that B.H.P. produces the
cheapest steel in the world.

Share farmers say the B,H.P's interlocked subsiduaries
produce the dearest agricultural machinery in the world, and
further they say the price rises not only with production costs,
but also whenever wheat value rises.

{{{page-break}}}

2\.

Many farmers could grow more wheat but their view is why
feed Australia and New Zealand at one third market value and not
ven receive full value for the exported balance, whilst on the
other hand they can get market value for wool mutton and beef?

Again take gold; if a man sweats his guts out around
Marble Bar, Bamboo Creek or Nullagine in an alluvial show, a leader
or a reef, he gets £10 an ounce for it, but if he can smuggle it
out of Broome or Darwin on a pearling lugger to Koepang, he can get
£30 an ounce for it.

Take kangaroo skins; one of the leading woolbrokers in
Australia told me personally that the price was controlled by one
man in Sydney ex U.S.A., who put the price up or down according to
whether there were too many kangarooers at work.

Sandalwood - in the grip of Plaimars. Some years ago I
located a patch of sandalwood and asked Wise M.L.A. Carnarvon, if
he would facilitate a permit for me to take it. Reply - China at
war, Plaimars cannot export, and are £90000 in arrears at the
Treasury with their royalties. "Nothing doing". Yet buyers in
Koepang were paying £30 a ton.

Wyndham meat works. No one in Wyndham can get work in the
meat works, all employment there is under the patronage of the
Secretary, A.W.U. Perth office. I pointed this out to an East
Kimberly Stock Inspector, his reply was "Quite right, the men
recruited in Perth go up to Wyndham from a temperate climate and
can work harder than the local men." Yet our Party pays lip
service to peopling the Far North.

Just now the Broome pearling is having an "open go",
but pre-war it was completely in the grip of an American monoply
which paid £160 per ton and limited the production.

As to copper I cannot speak personally, but am told by
a £2000 a year Mine manager, that it is, as far as Australia is
concerned entirely in the hands of the Bailleu Group. This is
not carping criticism, it is written in sorrow not in bitterness.

What I would suggest is that our Country Party and Liberal
leaders reduces their energies towards making political

{{{page-break}}}

-3-

capital of Labour's ignorance, selfishness and stubborness and
endeavour by honesty axl sincerity to win the support of the before
mentioned Moderate Vote.

One great fundamental truth greatly needs stressing,
i.e. that Capital in the final analysis is only another name for
thrift, that thrift, labour and enterprise spell prosperity.
That National Prosperity cannot be obtained by taking from the
provident to "hand out" to the improvident. That Capital is
a fruitful vine that only needs pruning, not destruction.

Let the Country Party and Liberal Party urge Premier
McLarty to promptly amend the Constitution of the Upper House
so that when Assembly has, after say two or even three General
Elections passed an enactment, the Council should be deprived of
the power of veto and thus demonstrate "Acta non verba",. As an
instance in point, the State Insurance Bill. Surely if the Improvident
are allowed a State Lottery Bill, the Provident as they
manifestly desire, should be allowed a State Insurance Bill.

In brief let us "rend our hearts and not our garments"
"Cast out the beam in thine own eye, then shalt thou see more
clearly to cast out the mote that is in thy brother's eye".

Why tolerate such meddlesome, petty fogging legislation
as the suppression of gifts in return for tinned milk and soap
labels, whilst there are so many important matters needing reform.
After all the Gift System is only an obscure form of time payment
and if a needy housewife can obtain a kitchen dresser on time
payment, why not a tea towel or a tea pot.

Finally isn't it fully time for us to muster our courage
and fight Sectarianism. The R.C. Church has sub rosa packed our
Parliaments, Civil Service and Education Department with its adherents,
although numerically they are less than one to three, yet we
are frightened to say so.

Whilst there was a preponderance of R.C. jockeys in the
saddle Labour could do no wrong according to the R.C. Church, but
now that some Communist jockeys are climbing into the saddle, the
R.C. Church grows critical of the Labour Party. In other words

{{{pge-break}}}

-4-

for Australia to be guided politically by an alien church in Italy
is good, but for Australia to be guided politically by an alien
political party in Russia is too horrible to contemplate.

Only that the Just would perish with the Unjust I could
wish that Chifley, like Blind Samson, might pull the National
Structure down upon his own head and those of his fellow traitors.

If you have followed me this far I feel that I have had
ten bob's worth of your time and patience.

I wish you God speed in your work, don't let obstacles
or disappointments discourage honest unselfish endeavour.

Truth is eternal and those who seek to destroy it perish
in the end.

Yours sincerely,\
(Signed) H.A. HALL.

CP/JMD.

St. George's House,\
115 St George's Tce.,
Perth

1st November 1947.

H. A. Hall Esq.,\
"Woopenatty,"\
{u|ARRINO.}

Dear Mr. Hall,

I must first of all apologise for not having acknowledged
your letter and your ver welcome donation before
this, but I have been out of town for a few days and with
other matters being rather pressing, I am behind in my correspondence.

I appreciate your letter very much, and may I say
that I wish there were a few more 'aged stockmen' amongst us.

I shall have pleasure in discussing your letter
with our Executive and with Mr. McLarty at the first opportunity.

In the meantime I hope you will let me write to you
more fully at a later date, saying at this stage that I agree
to a very great extent with all you have written.

I am enclosing a Membership Card, for you to sign
if you so desire. I hope you will. Official Receipt attached
and many thanks.

Cheerio for the present Mr. Hall,

Yours sincerely,\
C Palmer\
{u|General Secretary.}
